# Editorial and manuscript system

## Editing contract

Determine purpose, genre, audience, English variant, expected output, editing authority, source of truth, and protected passages. E0 is diagnosis; E1 is mechanics; E2 is copy/line edit; E3 is rewrite; E4 is restructure. A request to "proofread" does not authorize rewriting, and a request to "review" does not authorize replacing the author's prose.

Before editing, preserve the author's factual claims, names, numbers, dates, commitments, degree of certainty, citations, quotations, technical vocabulary, genre requirements, point of view, tense, and distinct rhetorical choices. Do not assume a phrase is wrong because it is uncommon. Diagnose its effect.

## Issue classification

Use the following distinctions, without inflating preference into error:

- **Mechanical error:** grammar, spelling, punctuation, agreement, typography, demonstrably incorrect usage.
- **Meaning risk:** ambiguous referent, accidental implication, lost qualification, incoherent chronology.
- **Reasoning or evidence problem:** non sequitur, missing premise, conflicting claims, insufficient support, overclaim.
- **Structural problem:** material appears in an order that defeats the reader's understanding, pacing, or causality.
- **Craft problem:** repetition, dead exposition, weak scene function, implausible dialogue, thin characterization.
- **Voice mismatch:** tone, register, rhythm, or persona conflicts with the identified author or destination.
- **Editorial preference:** a defensible alternative with a tradeoff, not a mandatory correction.

High-confidence corrections may be applied directly at the authorized level. Medium-confidence findings should be marked as questions or alternatives. Low-confidence findings require additional context or should be omitted.

For consequential audits, record issue ID, location, original excerpt, category, evidence, severity, impact, proposed change, confidence, and status. Group repeated manifestations into one systemic finding, with two or three examples rather than a hundred duplicate remarks.

## Revision dependency order

Correct meaning and factual/continuity contradictions before rearranging sections; correct structure before polishing individual sentences; correct paragraph logic before typography. A fine sentence should not be polished merely to be discarded in a later restructuring pass.

Favor minimum sufficient intervention. Preserve a strong original clause even when a smoother alternative exists. Check that changed pronouns, quantifiers, timelines, causal relations, negations, and citations remain valid. Compare before/after against the content spine, not just wording.

## Long-document protocol

1. Inventory the available chapters, sections, pages, or files. Distinguish inspected content from unavailable content.
2. Build a short reverse outline: function of each unit, central claim or story turn, supporting material, unresolved question, and links to adjacent units.
3. Build the smallest necessary ledgers: terminology and style sheet; entities and canon; timeline and knowledge states; claim and evidence map; sources and citations; open threads and promised payoffs.
4. Audit macrostructure and completeness first, then evidence and continuity, then voice and prose patterns, then mechanics.
5. Where a problem recurs, make one consistent editorial decision and apply it systematically.
6. Sample across beginnings, middles, endings, viewpoints, and argument types before asserting that a pattern is global.
7. Report coverage explicitly: **reviewed**, **sampled**, or **not reviewed**. Never imply a complete audit from disconnected excerpts.

A style sheet can track spellings, capitalization, hyphenation, conventions, transliteration, units, abbreviations, character names, preferred phrasing, citation style, and deliberate exceptions. Treat the style sheet as a record of the author's choices, not an excuse to homogenize expression.

## Consistency and revision comparison

For apparent contradictions, identify both source passages and determine whether they describe the same entity, time, scope, narrator, or measurement. A difference is not a contradiction until those dimensions align. For revision comparison, classify gains, regressions, and neutral alternatives separately; retain distinctive earlier language when the new version loses precision or voice.

Use publication-readiness judgments only for reviewed material: **ready**, **ready after targeted fixes**, **needs another revision pass**, or **needs structural revision**. State the smallest work remaining and the evidence for the verdict; do not predict publication, admission, or commercial outcomes.
