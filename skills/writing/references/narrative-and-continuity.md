# Narrative, structure, and continuity

## Story causality

Distinguish an actual plot contradiction from a motivation gap, missing setup, coincidence, convenience, unresolved question, withheld information, or reader misunderstanding. A plot hole requires established premises that make a necessary event impossible or incompatible with the story's rules. Cite the conflicting passages before labeling it.

For each major scene, identify the character's objective, obstacle, choice, consequential change, and connection to a later event. Scenes may be contemplative, atmospheric, or exploratory; their function need not be physical action. Diagnose the missing function rather than demanding a formula.

For pacing, identify why attention stalls or accelerates too quickly: repeated beats, static exposition, low consequence, missing reactions, delayed causality, abrupt transitions, premature explanation, or compressed emotional change. Recommend a specific operation: cut, combine, move, compress, dramatize, expand, delay, or intercut.

## Character and dialogue

Track desire, fear, practical motive, information available, agency, arc, relationships, and choices under pressure. A change in behavior needs psychological or situational support; it need not be predictable. Character distinctiveness is revealed by values, attention, choices, syntax, and what remains unsaid, not by arbitrary verbal quirks.

Assess dialogue for competing aims, subtext, authentic turn-taking, exposition disguised as speech, recognizable voices, tags and beats, and scene consequence. Preserve intentional dialect or nonstandard syntax. Do not force every exchange into witty repartee.

## Point of view, tense, and reader knowledge

Track narrator permissions and psychic distance. Distinguish intentional omniscience, free indirect discourse, and legitimate retrospective knowledge from accidental head-hopping. Record what each character and the reader knows at pivotal moments. A revelation fails when information appears without a plausible route to the knower, unless a defined narrative device permits it.

## Worldbuilding and setup/payoff

Document only the established rules, institutions, geography, technology, capabilities, resources, chronology, and consequences that influence the text. Ask how each rule constrains characters or events. Distinguish a rule broken on the page from a rule not yet specified. Track promises and foreshadowing as open threads, not mandatory checkboxes.

## Continuity ledger

For a long work, record each fact with source location and confidence:

- **Entities:** names, ages, attributes, relationships, possessions, injuries, roles, identifiers.
- **Timeline:** absolute and relative dates, travel durations, elapsed time, event order, simultaneous events.
- **Knowledge states:** who learned what, how, when; what they could infer; what remains secret.
- **World rules:** constraints, exceptions, mechanisms, institutions, vocabulary, consequences.
- **Open threads:** questions posed, promises made, setup, unresolved consequences, intended payoffs.

When conflicts appear, propose the least disruptive repair that protects intended character, theme, causality, and later chapters. A story bible is a source-backed ledger of established canon and open questions, not an invented encyclopedia.

## Narrative review output

For each important finding, provide scene or chapter location, the exact failure mode, evidence, reader consequence, and a practical repair. Separate structural fixes from prose suggestions. For a whole-book review, provide a reverse outline and dependency-ordered revision plan before a chapter-by-chapter mechanical edit.
