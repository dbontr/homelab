# Adaptive practice and difficult problem-solving

Practice makes remembered knowledge *deployable* from an unfamiliar situation. Solve mode develops original strategies on extended, difficult problems. Both are necessary but are not interchangeable.

## Practice cycle

1. Select the learner's target concept IDs from `get_graph`, `get_concept`, source-grounded reasoning or explicit user intent. Use `get_review_queue` and `get_reassessment_queue` to identify delayed obligations. Start or resume a session using `start_practice_session`, `list_practice_sessions` and `get_practice_session`.
2. Ask `recommend_practice` for the target, mode, difficulty and cue classes to avoid. The server orders weak or untested targets, interleaves topics, tracks failures requiring a fresh-context retest, and blocks a new problem while another remains open. Do not invent a progression score independently of that saved evidence.
3. Compose the problem yourself: make the applicable concept *not named in the statement*, choose a fresh surface, pre-solve it, check assumptions and search for unintended shortcuts. Call `create_practice_problem` with `session_id`, `target_concept_id`, `method`, a distinct `cue_class`, difficulty (1-5), `title`, `statement`, subject-appropriate `kind`, full `reference_solution` and explicit `grading_rubric`. A generated problem is not permitted without its private answer key.
4. Open `show_problem_workbench`. Let the learner work independently. They may submit multiple drafts, typed solutions or photographs. Preserve their actual work without rewriting it. Intervene only on explicit request in independent Solve mode, or at selected check points in Learn mode. A problem must not become a disguised vocabulary quiz. Encourage the learner to justify each important step and, when useful, report how confident they are (0–100%). Save an explicitly provided explanation and confidence with `save_problem_draft`; an unreported confidence remains unset. Evaluate the reasoning independently of the learner's stated confidence. Use `get_confidence_calibration` to compare certainty with precommitted answer keys and authenticated checks after sufficient objective observations. A high-confidence error warrants careful examination of the mistaken inference; a low-confidence success warrants reconstruction and delayed retesting rather than instant mastery. Do not use model-graded answers as ground truth for the calibration statistic.
5. For a hint, diagnose the earliest failure in selection or execution. Use this ladder with the minimum disclosure that moves work forward: R0 attention redirect, R1 pattern description, R2 candidate family, R3 name the proposition, R4 outline the move, R5 worked step. Give at most one rung per request and record the exact hint with `record_problem_hint`. Statement clarification is not a spoiler.
6. Classify misses: `cue`, `discrimination`, `execution`, `careless`, or `structural`. On an incorrect claimed final answer, locate the first invalid inference; do not automatically reveal the reference solution. Continue as long as the learner wishes. When finished, save an honest result using `finish_problem` and `record_practice_outcome`. Practice must count whether the learner needed assistance.
7. Require a new context for retests. `get_review_queue` shows scheduled delayed retrieval, and `get_reassessment_queue` identifies independent reconstruction that is at least 24 hours old and lacks qualifying later retention. The latest independent reconstruction resets the delay. Returning after an interval with a novel problem is stronger evidence than repeating an immediately revealed solution. Pause after a problem until the learner deliberately requests the next one.

For higher mastery, call `get_mastery_portfolio` rather than counting answers. Several distinct, genuinely challenging independent solutions, variety of cues and problem types, foundational reconstruction, and delayed reassessment are necessary. If a formal proof checker, symbolic tool, executable test, lab measurement or independent expert actually checked the solution, record its result and real artifact reference through `record_problem_check` as a **provisional claim**. Only a signed receipt from an operator-trusted checker, imported with `record_verified_problem_check`, can count as external corroboration. Do not invent or forge a checker result. A trusted signature establishes provenance, not a machine-verified theorem or a correct experimental conclusion.

## Educational safeguards

Do not confuse a learner's description of a proof with the ability to construct one. Do not assert that algebra by itself entails the calculus derivative: the limit construction introduces new definitions and assumptions. For empirical subjects, mastery may require a credible experiment, observation or material demonstration rather than a deductive proof. Preserve failures and partial solutions as useful evidence.
